## Revolutionary Extension 2: The Coordination Contagion

### Core Insight
Coordination spreads like a disease. Trust begets trust. Cooperation enables cooperation. Defection triggers defection.

### Epidemiological Model
$$\frac{dK_i}{dt} = \beta \sum_j A_{ij} K_j (K_{max} - K_i) - \gamma K_i + \eta_i$$

Where:
- $A_{ij}$ = Network adjacency (trade, treaties, communication)
- $\beta$ = Contagion rate (how fast cooperation spreads)
- $\gamma$ = Decay rate (how fast trust erodes without reinforcement)
- $\eta_i$ = Exogenous shocks

### Key Predictions
1. **Coordination Clusters**: High-K nations cluster geographically/politically
2. **Super-spreaders**: Some nations disproportionately affect global K
3. **Herd Immunity**: Above ~70% high-K nations, collapse becomes self-limiting
4. **Vaccination**: Strategic interventions in key nodes prevent cascades

## Revolutionary Extension 7: Coordination Genetics

### The Question
Why do some societies coordinate better than others? What are the "genes" of coordination?

### Candidate Factors
1. **Cultural**: Tight vs. loose cultures, individualism vs. collectivism
2. **Historical**: Colonial legacy, war trauma, institutional path dependence
3. **Geographic**: Resource distribution, climate, trade routes
4. **Linguistic**: Common language, translation infrastructure
5. **Religious**: Universal vs. tribal religions, reformation vs. not
6. **Technological**: Communication infrastructure, literacy rates

### The Coordination Genome Project
Map the factors that predict baseline coordination capacity across societies.

$$K_{baseline} = g(\text{Culture}, \text{History}, \text{Geography}, \text{Language}, \text{Religion}, \text{Technology})$$

### Policy Implication
If we understand the "genetics," we can design interventions that work with, not against, cultural predispositions.
