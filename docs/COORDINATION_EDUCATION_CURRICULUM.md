## Executive Summary

If coordination is infrastructure, we need people who know how to build it. This curriculum defines a comprehensive educational pathway from undergraduate awareness to doctoral research, creating the field of **Coordination Engineering Education**.

**Vision**: By 2040, every major university offers coordination courses, and 100,000 trained coordination engineers work across government, business, and civil society.

## 3. Course Catalog

### 3.1 Foundation Courses (All Pathways)

#### COORD 101: Introduction to Coordination Science
**Units**: 3 | **Prerequisites**: None

*The course that starts the journey.*

**Description**: What is coordination? Why does it matter? This course introduces the fundamental concepts of collective action, trust, and social cooperation. Students explore historical examples of coordination success and failure.

**Weekly Topics**:
1. What is Coordination? From Beehives to Boardrooms
2. The Coordination Paradox: Why Rational Individuals Make Irrational Groups
3. Game Theory Foundations: Prisoner's Dilemma to Stag Hunt
4. Trust: The Invisible Infrastructure
5. The Seven Harmonies Framework
6. Measuring Coordination: Introduction to the K-Index
7. Historical Coordination: From City-States to Nations
8. Modern Coordination Challenges: Climate, Pandemics, AI
9. Coordination Across Cultures: East vs West
10. The Future of Coordination: Technology and Trust

**Assessment**:
- Weekly reflections (20%)
- Group coordination exercise (30%)
- Final essay: "A coordination failure I've witnessed" (25%)
- Peer assessment (25%)

---

#### COORD 201: The K-Index: Theory and Application
**Units**: 4 | **Prerequisites**: COORD 101

*The quantitative heart of coordination science.*

**Description**: Deep dive into the K-Index methodology. Students learn to calculate, interpret, and apply the seven harmonies framework. Includes hands-on data analysis.

**Weekly Topics**:
1. From Concept to Measurement: The K-Index Origins
2. Harmony 1: Governance - Measuring Institutional Quality
3. Harmony 2: Interconnection - Networks and Flows
4. Harmony 3: Trust - The Binding Constraint
5. Harmony 4: Complexity - Diversity Without Fragmentation
6. Harmony 5: Knowledge - Epistemic Infrastructure
7. Harmony 6: Wellbeing - The Human Foundation
8. Harmony 7: Capability - Technical and Operational
9. Aggregation: The Geometric Mean and Why It Matters
10. Data Sources and Quality Assessment
11. The Golden Threshold (θ = 0.382) and Phase Transitions
12. Case Study: Using K-Index for Country Assessment

**Lab Component**:
- Weekly data analysis assignments using Python
- Students compute K-Index for assigned countries
- Final project: Original K-Index analysis

---

#### COORD 202: Trust Engineering
**Units**: 3 | **Prerequisites**: COORD 101

*Building the invisible infrastructure.*

**Description**: Trust is the binding constraint on coordination. This course explores how trust forms, why it breaks, and how to rebuild it. Combines psychology, sociology, and engineering perspectives.

**Weekly Topics**:
1. What is Trust? Definitions and Dimensions
2. Trust Formation: From Strangers to Partners
3. Institutional Trust vs Interpersonal Trust
4. Trust Metrics: Measuring the Unmeasurable
5. Trust Destruction: Betrayal, Deception, and Erosion
6. Trust Repair: Apology, Restitution, and Time
7. Designing for Trust: Architecture and Incentives
8. Verification Systems: Trust but Verify
9. Cross-Cultural Trust: Different Foundations
10. Digital Trust: Algorithms, Platforms, and Privacy

**Practical Component**:
- Trust-building simulation exercises
- Analysis of trust failure case studies
- Design project: Trust mechanism for assigned context

### 3.2 Core Methods Courses

#### COORD 301: Coordination Diagnostics
**Units**: 4 | **Prerequisites**: COORD 201

*Finding the root cause of coordination failures.*

**Description**: How do you diagnose why a group, organization, or nation is failing to coordinate? This course teaches systematic diagnostic methods using the K-Index framework.

**Diagnostic Framework**:
```
Step 1: Symptom Identification
└── What coordination failures are observable?

Step 2: K-Index Assessment
└── Calculate overall K and harmony breakdown

Step 3: Gap Analysis
├── Which harmony is weakest?
├── How does it compare to peers?
└── What is the historical trajectory?

Step 4: Root Cause Analysis
├── Why is this harmony weak?
├── What structural factors contribute?
└── What triggered recent changes?

Step 5: Intervention Design
├── What could address root causes?
├── What are second-order effects?
└── How do we measure success?
```

---

#### COORD 302: Institutional Design for Coordination
**Units**: 4 | **Prerequisites**: COORD 201

*Building the structures that enable cooperation.*

**Description**: Institutions shape coordination capacity. This course teaches principles of institutional design that promote collective action, from team charters to international treaties.

**Design Principles**:
1. **Clarity**: Clear rules, roles, and expectations
2. **Enforcement**: Credible consequences for defection
3. **Flexibility**: Adaptation without collapse
4. **Legitimacy**: Perceived fairness and buy-in
5. **Transparency**: Information flow that builds trust
6. **Inclusivity**: Voice for affected parties
7. **Scalability**: Works at different sizes

**Projects**:
- Redesign a failing organization's governance
- Draft a model treaty for emerging technology
- Create coordination charter for community initiative

---

#### COORD 303: Intervention Design and Implementation
**Units**: 4 | **Prerequisites**: COORD 301, COORD 302

*From diagnosis to action.*

**Description**: Designing interventions is one thing; implementing them is another. This course covers the full lifecycle of coordination interventions, from conception to evaluation.

**Intervention Lifecycle**:

```
CONCEPTION ──► DESIGN ──► PILOT ──► SCALE ──► EVALUATE ──► ITERATE

     ▼            ▼         ▼         ▼           ▼            ▼
 Stakeholder   Theory of  Small-n   Expand     Rigorous    Feedback
 analysis      change     test      carefully   assessment   loop
```

### COORD 101: Introduction to Coordination Science

**Instructor**: TBD
**Time**: MWF 10:00-10:50
**Location**: Room TBD

**Course Description**:
This course introduces the science of coordination—how groups of people (from teams to nations to humanity) solve collective action problems. We explore why rational individuals often fail to cooperate, how trust enables coordination, and what institutions and interventions can build coordination capacity.

**Learning Objectives**:
By the end of this course, students will be able to:
1. Define coordination and distinguish it from cooperation and collaboration
2. Explain the fundamental coordination problems (prisoner's dilemma, tragedy of commons)
3. Describe the role of trust in enabling coordination
4. Apply the seven harmonies framework to analyze coordination contexts
5. Identify coordination failures in historical and contemporary examples
6. Propose basic interventions to improve coordination

- *Coordination Engineering: A New Science for a New Century* (forthcoming)
- Course packet with primary readings
