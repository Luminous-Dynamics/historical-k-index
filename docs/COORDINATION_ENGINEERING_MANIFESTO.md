# The Coordination Engineering Manifesto

**A Call for a New Discipline**

**Version**: 1.0.0
**Created**: December 10, 2025

---

## Preamble

We stand at a crossroads. Humanity faces challenges that no nation, no organization, no individual can solve alone. Climate change. Pandemic preparedness. AI governance. Nuclear security. Biodiversity collapse.

These are not problems of knowledge - we know what needs to be done.
These are not problems of resources - we have the wealth and technology.
These are **problems of coordination** - and we lack the science to solve them.

We have physics for matter. Biology for life. Economics for markets.

**We have no engineering discipline for coordination.**

Until now.

---

## I. The Problem

### The Coordination Deficit

```
         CAPABILITY                 COORDINATION
         GROWTH                     GROWTH
           ▲                          ▲
           │     ╱                    │
           │   ╱                      │    ____
           │ ╱                        │   /
           │                          │  /
           │                          │ /
           │                          │/
           └──────────────► Time      └──────────────► Time

        EXPONENTIAL                 LINEAR (at best)
```

Our technical capabilities grow exponentially. Our ability to coordinate grows linearly - if at all.

This gap is the source of nearly every existential risk we face:
- Nuclear weapons exist because physics advanced faster than trust
- Climate change persists because emissions coordination lags
- Pandemics spread because health coordination is ad hoc
- AI races ahead because governance coordination is absent

### The Missing Science

We have:
- **Engineering** to build things
- **Economics** to allocate resources
- **Political Science** to understand power
- **Sociology** to describe groups
- **Psychology** to understand individuals

We lack:
- **A rigorous science of collective action**
- **Predictive models of coordination dynamics**
- **Engineering principles for building coordination capacity**
- **Measurement tools for coordination health**
- **Intervention frameworks for coordination failures**

This is the gap that Coordination Engineering fills.

## II. The Vision

### What is Coordination Engineering?

**Coordination Engineering** is the discipline of designing, building, measuring, and repairing systems of collective action.

Just as:
- Civil engineering builds bridges
- Software engineering builds programs
- Financial engineering builds instruments

**Coordination engineering builds cooperation.**

### Core Principles

1. **Coordination is Measurable**
   - The K-Index provides a scientific measurement framework
   - Seven harmonies decompose coordination into components
   - Historical data enables prediction and intervention

2. **Coordination is Designable**
   - Institutions can be designed to build coordination
   - Incentives can be aligned to promote cooperation
   - Information flows can be optimized for collective action

3. **Coordination is Buildable**
   - Like any infrastructure, coordination capacity can be constructed
   - Investment in coordination yields returns
   - Coordination compounds over time

4. **Coordination is Repairable**
   - Failed coordination can be diagnosed
   - Specific harmonies can be targeted
   - Trust can be rebuilt systematically

### Case Study: Climate Coordination Engineering

**Problem**: Climate action requires unprecedented global coordination

**Diagnosis**:
- Global K ≈ 0.65 (capable but struggling)
- H₃ (Trust) is lowest harmony at 0.52
- H₂ (Interconnection) is high at 0.78
- Governance gap: H₇ (0.82) - H₃ (0.52) = 0.30

**Analysis**:
- Trust is the binding constraint
- Technical capability exists
- Communication networks exist
- Governance institutions are weak

**Design Interventions**:
1. **Trust-Building**
   - Climate finance transparency registry
   - Third-party emissions verification
   - Loss and damage mechanism (demonstrated commitment)

2. **Institution Strengthening**
   - Binding enforcement mechanisms
   - Independent scientific arbitration
   - Tiered obligations by capability

3. **Incentive Alignment**
   - Carbon border adjustments (make defection costly)
   - Technology transfer incentives (make cooperation pay)
   - Early mover advantages for leaders

**Implementation**:
- Pilot with high-K coalition (EU, UK, Canada, Singapore)
- Demonstrate effectiveness
- Expand to medium-K nations with support
- Bring low-K nations along with capacity building

**Monitor**:
- Track K-Index of participating nations
- Measure trust indicators quarterly
- Adjust interventions based on results

## V. The Call

### To Researchers

We call on scholars across disciplines to join in building the science of coordination:
- **Game theorists**: Model coordination dynamics with empirical grounding
- **Network scientists**: Map coordination pathways and bottlenecks
- **Institutional economists**: Design incentive-compatible cooperation
- **Political scientists**: Understand power dynamics in collective action
- **Psychologists**: Study trust formation and repair
- **Sociologists**: Analyze social capital and norms
- **Computer scientists**: Build coordination support tools

### To Practitioners

We call on practitioners to adopt the coordination engineering mindset:
- **Policymakers**: Design policies with coordination impact in mind
- **Business leaders**: Measure and build organizational coordination
- **Civil society**: Advocate for coordination infrastructure investment
- **Diplomats**: Apply K-Index thinking to international negotiations
- **Civic leaders**: Build urban coordination capacity

### To Funders

We call on funders to invest in coordination infrastructure:
- **Research funding**: Support coordination science development
- **Capacity building**: Fund K-Index improvement programs
- **Tool development**: Build coordination measurement platforms
- **Education**: Train the next generation of coordination engineers
- **Policy pilots**: Test coordination interventions

### To All

We call on everyone to recognize:
- Coordination is infrastructure, as essential as roads or networks
- Coordination capacity can be built deliberately
- Investment in coordination has compounding returns
- Our collective future depends on coordination capacity

## VI. The Future

### The Coordinated World (2050)

Imagine a world where:
- **Global coordination capacity** is measured annually, like GDP
- **Coordination engineering** is a recognized profession
- **Trust infrastructure** is invested in like physical infrastructure
- **Institutional design** is evidence-based
- **Collective action problems** have systematic solutions

Where:
- Climate action is coordinated because trust mechanisms work
- Pandemic response is swift because cooperation structures exist
- AI governance is effective because capacity was built in advance
- Conflict is rare because coordination is cheaper than confrontation

This world is possible. The science exists. The need is clear.

What we lack is the decision to build it.

### The Choice

We face two futures:

**Path A: Coordination Failure**
- Technology races ahead of governance
- Trust erodes as coordination fails
- Collective action problems multiply
- Civilization struggles with preventable crises
- K-Index stagnates or declines

**Path B: Coordination Engineering**
- Deliberate investment in coordination capacity
- Trust infrastructure built systematically
- Institutions designed for collective action
- Problems solved through cooperation
- K-Index rises toward 1.0

The difference between these paths is not destiny. It is choice.

We choose Path B. We choose Coordination Engineering.
